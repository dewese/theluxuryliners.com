## 1. Summary

- **What the band is.** The Luxury Liners are the band **David Dewese co-founded with his college friend Chad Edgington in 1997**. They took it to **Nashville**, where it became a sharp-dressed guitar power-pop band (`DEC` F4, P21; confirmed-owner). The name comes from **Gram Parsons' song "Luxury Liner"** (AllMusic bio as reprinted on the band's 2005 press page; David in *earpollution* 2001; verified-source).
- **From alt-country to power pop.** AllMusic (Erik Hage, 2001): the band was "originally intended as an alt-country outlet for Foxymorons member David Dewese … the group ended up more as a guitar-crunchy power pop band that called to mind classic acts like Big Star, Badfinger, and the Raspberries" (`R04` §5.2, press page `WB:20051225072849/press.html`; verified-source). David's own version (2001): "Then we discovered the Beach Boys and the alt-country dream was over for the Luxury Liners" (`R05` §7; verified-source as David's claim).
- **Forever members** (`DEC` P8; confirmed-owner): **David Dewese, Chad Edgington, Scott Carpenter** (drums, from August 1998) and **David "Larry" Wilstermann** (bass, from December 2000). He was nicknamed "Larry" because the band already had a David.
- **Chad left Nashville in 2001; David carried the band on** (`DEC` F4; confirmed-owner). The month is contested: March or April 2001 (§10, D3).
- **Records:** *Sound As Ever* (2000), the *Believe* EP (2001), *Overbored* (2003), *Nonetheless* (2006), "Shake It Up (Live at the Texas Music Café)" (2021), "Great Day" (2026-02-13) and "New Beginning" (2026-04-17). Also a 2026 remastered vinyl reissue of *Sound As Ever* on Sweden's Sound Asleep Records (`R01` §2; `DEC` F6, P6; verified-source / confirmed-owner).
- **Peak years: 1998–2004.** The band was named one of *Billboard*'s "Hot Prospects" in 1998 (BS snippet; the 2000 band bio calls it a "buzz-bin article"; the original is unseen; unverified). They played the **Monsters of Pop** showcase in Nashville in June 1998 (NS-snip; single-source), were on two 1998 compilations, and had a CD handed out at a **SESAC showcase at SXSW 2001** (Discogs via `R01` §4; verified-source). Between 2001 and 2004 they logged about 40 shows and trips in their own journal: Memphis, Las Vegas (House of Blues), Atlanta (opening for Blues Traveler), Detroit, Indiana, South Carolina, Kentucky, Washington DC and many Nashville clubs (`R04` §5.2; verified-source as the band's own account).
- **On screen:** "Dreaming" was used on *One Tree Hill* (S1 E15), and "Sunshine" became the theme of FOX Sports' *US Youth Soccer Show* (`R04` §4.2 placements page; verified-source; air dates unverified).
- **Dormancy and return.** After *Nonetheless* (2006) the trio still played into 2007 (photos `PH`). David moved to Southern California in late 2010. By 2012 the band site said "The band is currently going by the name 'David Dewese.'" (`R04` §5.1 L6). David has written: "Although the band members are now scattered across four different states I refuse to say we've broken up." (`R04` §4.2, 2012–24 bio). The band came back with a live single in 2021 and two new singles in 2026 (`DEC` F6).
- **Biggest gaps:** the band's own concert history page (`/history/concerts.html`, archived 2003) and the 2000 news/events pages were never read. The 1997–2000 show history is therefore almost empty. Also missing: who plays on the 2026 singles, when the 2021 live track was recorded, and the original *Billboard* item (§11).

## 2. Origins (1997): two Texans, a Gram Parsons song and a move to Nashville

### 2.1 The authoritative version (use this in copy)

> In 1997 **David Dewese and his college friend Chad Edgington co-founded The Luxury Liners** and took the band to Nashville. (`DEC` F4, P21; confirmed-owner. P21 confirms "college friend Chad Edgington" as the right wording.)

David told it this way on his own site, 2012–2024: *"I moved to Nashville with my college buddy, Chad Edgington, to start this band. We played a zillion shows, made records, and met famous people."* (`R04` §4.2, [daviddewese.com /about/ 20130901004222](https://web.archive.org/web/20130901004222/http://daviddewese.com/about/); verified-source.)

The Foxymorons oral history (Carly, 2026-10-07) agrees: *"Spring 1997: David moved to Nashville to start a working band, The Luxury Liners, with a college friend."* (`OH-FM`; confirmed-owner.)

**Owner account, 2026-10-07 (`DECISIONS-2026-10-07.md` LL-4; confirmed-owner; authoritative).** Chad and David corresponded by email in the winter and spring of 1997, while Chad was finishing college and David was living in Mesquite, Texas. They came up with the band name over spring break 1997, when they met up to record a ton of songs together on a 4-track. They moved to Nashville in **May 1997**, after Chad's graduation. This matches the 2005 roster (David from May 1997) and David's own "I moved to Nashville with my college buddy … to start this band". It does **not** match the 2005 site's talent-show account of the name (below), which stays archive lore only (Q1/B1). (The place of the spring-break sessions is private and is never named.)

### 2.3 The name and the "Texas Pop" idea

- **Namesake:** Gram Parsons' song "Luxury Liner", first recorded by Parsons' International Submarine Band on *Safe at Home* (1968) and later the title track of Emmylou Harris's 1977 album. AllMusic 2001: "Named after a Gram Parsons song". David, 2001: *"When I moved to Nashville, I named my band after a Gram Parsons song called 'Luxury Liner.'"* (`R05` §7, *earpollution*, Aug 2001, on [foxymorons.com bio 20030211083145](https://web.archive.org/web/20030211083145/http://www.foxymorons.com:80/bio.html)). Verified-source. **Note:** David's wording ("I named my band") is his 2001 claim. Copy should say the band was named after the song, not who named it, until Carly says otherwise.
- **Gram Parsons threads in the catalog** (an observation, not a source claim; unverified as intent):
  - *Sound As Ever*: Gram Parsons signed his letters "Sound as ever" (`R05` §4.7 note).
  - *Sound As Ever* ends with a hidden track called "The International Submarine Band", the name of Parsons' first band (`R01` §2 tracklist).
  - The band played a surprise cover of "Luxury Liner" in East Nashville on 2003-10-18 (`R04` §5.2 journal).
- **The Nudie-suit years.** David, 2001: *"We got to meet [Western clothes designer] Manuel and wear Nudie Suits in concert. We met Emmylou [Harris] and Philip Kaufman… They all came to see us play."* (`R05` §7; verified-source **as David's claim**; not independently confirmed). Supporting photos: a 1997 suit ("Rocking the '97 suit", `PH:3978343931`, 2009); the white-studio jumping and suit photos by Mark Montgomery (c. 2000, `PH:2074031888`, `PH:4471053728`); and the *Shake It Up (Live)* cover, with three members mid-jump in embroidered Western suits (`R02` cover table).
- **"Texas Pop".** The band's own 2005 phrase (above). The 2001 *Nashville Rage* called them *"Former Texans who craft stylish Americana pop as if the Byrds had arisen in a post-alternative rock world"* (press page; verified-source).

## 4. Eras

### 4.1 1997–1999: arrival in Nashville, the first records, *Billboard*

- **Nashville, 1997.** David and Chad move to Nashville in (spring) 1997 (`DEC` F4; `OH-FM`). David's 2010 Flickr caption: *"I moved to Nashville 13 years ago to make music. Mission accomplished!"* (`PH:5122724227`, 2010-10-27; verified-source). The band's photo-archive filenames, archived in 2005, hint at what has not been read yet: `97_12th_porter.jpg`, `97_opry.jpg`, `97_pajamas.jpg`, `98_hard_rock.jpg`, `98_waco.jpg`, `99_12th_serious.jpg`, `99_green_jackets.jpg`, `99_session_guitars.jpg` (`CDX`, `/history/photos/`). That suggests 1997 shows at 12th & Porter and something at or near the Opry, and a 1998 Hard Rock show. These are filename inferences only (unverified; open in a browser, §11).
- **Monsters of Pop, June 11–13, 1998.** A three-night pop showcase at the Exit/In and The End. On the bill: Marshall Crenshaw with Bill Lloyd, Japan's Hicksville, Will Owsley, Swan Dive, The Shazam, Neilson Hubbard, Who Hit John, Joe Marc's Brother, Betty Rocker, Rayon City Quartet, Ross Rice, the Idle Jets, Paul Myers, Cherry Twister and **"the Byrds-influenced Luxury Liners"**. Organiser: Lee Swartz (P.O.P. UN Ltd.). (NS-snip: *Nashville Scene* "Top of the Pops" and "Block-Rockin'", [link 1](https://www.nashvillescene.com/arts_culture/top-of-the-pops/article_a7f15ca7-0558-526a-9709-e4ee4a74a05f.html), [link 2](https://www.nashvillescene.com/arts_culture/block-rockin/article_81f0b3b8-63a9-521b-82c4-d324fe5d9065.html); single-source; the year 1998 comes from the snippet and must be checked.) By the 2005 roster, the drummer then was Kyle Edgington (May–Aug 1998).
- ***Billboard*, 1998.** The Baptist Standard says the band was "named one of Billboard magazine's Hot Prospects in 1998" (BS snippet). The 2000 band bio says their live show earned "a buzz-bin article in industry giant Billboard magazine" (`WB:20000926003543/bio.htm`). David's Flickr has a "Billboard Photo Shoot 1998" set of contact sheets (`PH:2068108482`). Confidence: **unverified**. Two band-side sources agree, but the *Billboard* item itself has not been seen (look in the worldradiohistory.com Billboard 1998 PDFs or Google Books, §11).
- **1998 compilations** (`R01` §4; verified-source):
  - *Fireworks Vol. 2: 25 Explosive Tracks* (Sound Asleep Records, Sweden, ZZZ006): "Since You Met Me" and "Think She's Coming Around"; line-up David/Chad/Jeff LaFrate; produced by Mike Poole.
  - *Nashpop (A Nashville Pop Compilation)* (Not Lame Recordings NL-046): "If I Cry" (Edgington/Dewese), a song on no Luxury Liners album.
- ***Trunk Box*, fall 1998.** A live MP3 album from E-Cleff Studios, Waco, Texas, described as "scott's first offical gig". It has 11 tracks, including songs heard nowhere else: "Trans-Am Mind", "Araby" and (per the archived MP3 paths) "Be With You", "Blockbuster", "If I Cry", "Shake It Up" and "Say Goodbye" (`R04` §5.2; `CDX` `/mp3/waco/…`). Verified-source (archive); never released commercially. It is a Vault item (P5).
- **"Blockbuster".** One of the first songs David and Jerry James wrote for the Foxymorons. *"Jerry hated it, but the Luxury Liners have played it for four years now"* (David, Aug 2001; `R05` §7). That puts it in the set from about 1997.
- **Production deal.** The Baptist Standard says the band "secured a production deal, recorded two CDs" (BS snippet). The two CDs fit *Sound As Ever* (2000) and the *Believe* EP (2001), both produced by Mark Montgomery and released on echomusic. **That the deal was with Montgomery/echomusic is an inference** (unverified; Q4).

### 4.2 2000: *Sound As Ever*

- **Release:** May 2000 (public date 2000-05-01, Spotify, P6). Label: Echomusic / Litterbug Records, cat. EMLLCD1001. 15 tracks, the last three hidden ("three unreleased hidden bonus tracks", band site). Produced by **Mike Poole** (recorded and mixed in Nashville) and **Mark Montgomery** (also design). Written and performed by David Dewese and Chad Edgington, with Scott Carpenter performing (`R01` §2; verified-source).
- **Songs:** "Think She's Coming Around", "Fresh Start", "Just A Girl", "Since You Met Me", "When We're Alone", "The Duke Of Gloucester", "Pecan Valley Blues", "Breezy", "Mine", "Serengeti Summer", "User's Guide", "How Do I Say Goodbye", then hidden: "Precious" ("Precious To My Heart"), "The Loneliest Boy In Town" and "The International Submarine Band".
- **"The Duke Of Gloucester"** is on both the Foxymorons' *Calcutta* (1999) and *Sound As Ever*. David, 2001: *"Both bands even recorded one song called 'The Duke of Gloucester.'"* (`R05` §7). Who wrote it is an open question (QUESTIONS-FOR-CARLY Q7).
- **2000 bio** (`WB:20000926003543/bio.htm`): *"How many times do you come across a rock band whose ultimate career goal is a caricature lunch box? Meet the Luxury Liners, an original pop band with the drive and potential to conquer the world, armed with all the spontaneity and charm of a 3-piece rock combo."* Scott: *"We always try to look sharp when we hit the stage, to show the audience we take a lot of pride in what we're doing."* (verified-source; this page also prints an old mailing address and phone number, so **do not republish the page whole**.)
- **The Foxymorons' first show (SXSW, March 2000).** Chad drove down from Nashville with David to play it (`OH-FM`). The Foxymorons history lists Chad (guitar/bass) and Scott (drums) as live Foxymorons players in 2000–2002 (`R05` §3). This is a key link between the two bands.

## 8. The Nashville scene, and how the band connects to The Foxymorons and David's solo work

- **The scene.** In the late 1990s Nashville had a non-country pop and power-pop scene with its own showcases (Lee Swartz's Monsters of Pop), its own compilation (*Nashpop*, Not Lame 1998) and local press that covered it (*Nashville Scene*, *Nashville Rage*, *The Tennessean*) (NS-snip; `R01`). The Luxury Liners were among its "Byrds-influenced" acts (1998) and "the best (and best dressed) purveyors of pop in Nashville" (*Nashville Rage* 7-02). Another Nashville band, The Luxury Stars, reportedly took care to avoid confusion with "fellow Nashville power-poppers The Luxury Liners" (NS-snip, "Formation of Stars"; single-source).
- **The Foxymorons.** David's other band, co-founded with Jerry James (`DEC` F5). The two bands overlapped from 1997 to 2006, and the members swapped:
  - Chad and Scott played live Foxymorons shows (2000–02).
  - Songs moved between bands: "The Duke Of Gloucester" (both bands recorded it); "Blockbuster" (a Foxymorons-era song the LL played); "Baby Blue" (an LL reject that became a Foxymorons song).
  - David's 2001 rule of thumb: *"Luxury Liners has the poppy girl/boy songs, and Foxymorons has the weird art songs. Lately the lines have been blurring… Each band also gets a chance at the other band's rejects"* (`R05` §7).
  - Mike Poole produced both bands' first albums. Sound Asleep Records released both bands' compilation tracks.
- **David's solo work.** David played his first solo show in September 2001, right after Chad left, with a set that mixed LL and Foxymorons songs. His solo albums came out on Litterbug from 2008. Bandmates appear on them: Wilstermann plays bowed bass on *Make The Best Of It* and is a "special guest elf" on *Next Christmas* (`R04` §4.2). The LL catalog sits on David's own Bandcamp (daviddewese.bandcamp.com) and shares his ISRC registrant (USQ7J) (`R01`).
- **Sideman links.** David toured and recorded with The Nobility, the former Jetpack, who shared LL bills (`DEC` P9). Scott drummed for Ginny Owens on tour (2002 journal).

## 9. Anecdotes and lore (from the band's own site and journal)

All are verified-source **as the band's own words**. Quote only with Carly's OK, and check each passage for private details first.

1. **The talent show.** In the 2005 band story, the name began with a college talent-show band that "did indeed win the contest". The band then "invented 'Texas Pop'" and moved to Nashville "to 'get famous'". (Copy rule: F4 framing first; §2.)
2. **The caricature lunch box.** The 2000 bio's "ultimate career goal is a caricature lunch box".
3. **Matching gray slacks from Castner-Knott**, worn at Chad's last show, March 2001 (`PH:530769478`). Castner-Knott was a Nashville department store.
4. **"Larry."** The band called David Wilstermann "Larry" because there was already a David (`DEC` P8). There was a "LARRY" T-shirt (2007), and the Flickr set "ARCHIVE: Luxury Larry Promos".
5. **Las Vegas:** "all of nevada was rocked last friday night" (journal, June 2002).
6. **"We didn't win"**: a 2002 battle of the bands, a neat echo of the talent-show origin (journal, July 2002).
7. **The money:** the first out-of-region trip, Ohio and Michigan in Sept 2001: "we lost a little money".
8. **A surprise cover of "Luxury Liner"** for an East Nashville crowd, Oct 2003.
9. **Nudie suits, Manuel, Emmylou and Phil Kaufman** (David, 2001; unconfirmed).
10. **"Rocking the '97 suit"** in 2009 (`PH:3978343931`).
11. **"I refuse to say we've broken up"** (David, 2012–24).
12. **"Forever brosephs"** (the current site, 2024).
13. **Chad's rejected solo album cover** (`PH:529309403`). **Do not use**: it shows Chad's likeness and is outside the band's story (`R06` rights flag).
